## Challenge

Reef keepers already had forums, spreadsheets, camera rolls, and a local fish store that disappears the moment livestock leaves the shop. None of those share a tank. A post on a forum rewrites the setup every time. A logbook app has no community. A store has no view of the aquarium after the sale.

I built Reefly to put the tank in the middle: parameters, livestock, routines, and a community that can see the setup the question is about. It is free for hobbyists, and the store side is built in rather than bolted on later.

## The product

[reeflycare.com](https://reeflycare.com/) opens with that claim: Reef 2 Reef, replaced. Free for hobbyists. Built for fish stores. The hero is a Today card — tank name, a health score, nitrate, and the water change that is due — sitting next to the stores already on the platform.

The product page is the same idea without the manifesto. Tracking, community, and Reef Agent live in one place. A tank profile holds parameters, livestock, water changes, and the week’s chores, and it grows with the aquarium instead of resetting every time someone asks for help.

![Reefly product — tank tracking with alkalinity, nitrate, phosphate, livestock, and the week’s chores](/images/work/reefly/product-hero.jpg)

On a signed-in tank that looks like a 90 gallon mixed reef: alkalinity, nitrate, and phosphate on one card; clownfish, a yellow tang, and a torch coral on the livestock list; Saturday’s water change and Sunday’s glass-and-test on the routine. Reef Talk sits beside it, so a nitrate question can carry the tank, the reading, and the replies in the same view.

![My Tanks next to a Reef Talk thread that already knows the gallonage and the nitrate reading](/images/work/reefly/tanks.jpg)

Reef Agent is the plain-language layer on top of that log. A keeper can ask to record a water change, ask why a coral closed up, or draft a community post, and the answer is tied to their tanks when they are signed in. Photo check, care history, and routines stay on the tank rather than in a separate notes app.

## Community that knows the setup

Reef Talk is the forum: Forums, Builds, Chemistry, Swaps, and Market, with marine and freshwater rooms, search, and threads that can name the tank they came from. The public comparison on the homepage is the product decision in a table. Reef2Reef is built around threads. Reefly is built around the tank. The setup stays on the tank instead of being rewritten in every post. Tracking is parameters, livestock, and chores. Community is Reef Talk with the tank attached. It is phone-first, and it is free for hobbyists.

## Fish stores

The other half of the company is the shop that sold the livestock. Store Link is how a store stays in the picture after a customer walks out with a coral or a whole setup. The store sends an invite. The hobbyist accepts and chooses which tanks to share. The store sees parameters and livestock on those tanks and can message inside Reefly. Linking is opt-in, and the hobbyist can unlink.

![Store Link — invite, accept, see the tank](/images/work/reefly/stores-hero.jpg)

That was the early go-to-market as well. The first partner store was Gio Marine in the Bay Area, then a wider roster of local and online shops listed on the homepage. The public page for shops is [reeflycare.com/for-stores](https://reeflycare.com/for-stores).

## What I shipped

I founded it and built the consumer product end to end: the marketing site, tank tools, Reef Talk, Reef Agent, and the store side, plus an iOS companion ([Reefly-IOS](https://github.com/rarulraj/Reefly-IOS)). The live app is [reeflycare.com](https://reeflycare.com/). It launched in public in April 2026, first with a few dozen users and one shop, then with tanks, posts, and a partner roster on the homepage.

## Outcome

Reefly was acquired by an independent business owner after the core surfaces were in market. The case is a full 0 to 1: a free hobbyist product, a store workflow, a community that carries tank context, and a clean ownership handoff.
