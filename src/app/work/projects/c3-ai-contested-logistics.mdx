## The problem the product is for

Contested logistics is the supply problem where the map is not stable. Routes, inventory, and demand still have to be planned, but the environment can take options away. [C3 AI Contested Logistics](https://c3.ai/products/applications/c3-ai-contested-logistics) is the application built for that: a resilient supply network with near-real-time visibility, and AI that puts the risks in an order a planner can act on.

I was a software engineer on the Fed Apps side of C3 AI from June 2024 to September 2025, on that product context and on the agentic internal tools the teams used to build and operate AI applications.

## What the application surface is

The public product is a stack from data to a picture a commander or a planner can use. The capabilities C3 lists are the ones the engineering work sat under:

- A logistics common operating picture, and visibility into global supply inventory
- Generative AI across enterprise sources that do not share a schema
- Demand forecasting and scenario-based planning
- Executive dashboards that synthesize, rather than dump, the operational picture
- Scenario analysis that can run an exercise and show how a logistics chain reacts
- Logistics supportability — can this plan actually be sustained
- Campaign and contingency planning for combined, joint operations in contested conditions
- Inventory in the right place at the right time

That is a data problem before it is a dashboard problem. Inventory, demand, and risk live in different systems. The application has to join them, score what matters, and keep the picture current enough that a scenario is about today’s network and not last quarter’s extract. C3’s own description of the platform, from people who deploy it, is the span I worked inside: data layer, analytic layer, visualization layer.

The product holds “Awardable” status in the Chief Digital and Artificial Intelligence Office’s Tradewinds Solutions Marketplace. That is the federal path the application sits on. It is also why the engineering bar is production software, not a prototype that only works on a curated extract.

## My role

On Fed Apps I built enterprise AI applications and deployed predictive models into that kind of environment. The day-to-day was the unglamorous part of making an AI application real: data in, a model or an agent that can be run again, a surface someone outside the team can use, and DevOps that does not depend on one engineer’s laptop.

The other half of the seat was **agentic AI internal tools**. The goal was speed. Teams building AI products should not hand-stitch the same prompting, evaluation, and operations steps for every application. Internal tooling that encodes those steps is how a Fed Apps group ships more than one system without inventing a new pipeline each time. I contributed to that tooling and used it on the logistics work.

I do not publish customer environments, data, or internal architecture from that seat. The public product page is the right depth for what the application does. The engineering claim I will make is the one on the role: scalable enterprise AI, predictive ML in production, and agentic tools that shortened how the team built.

## Outcome

A year-plus of enterprise AI delivery on C3’s platform, in a product line aimed at supply networks that have to keep working when conditions change. Data, analytics, and visualization in one application, plus internal agent tooling that made the next application faster to stand up. The product write-up is [C3 AI Contested Logistics](https://c3.ai/products/applications/c3-ai-contested-logistics).
